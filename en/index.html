Brenda Locke

Surrey Connect

MAIN PROMISES
Aquatic centres, public safety campus[15]
BACKGROUND
Incumbent mayor; says she has built the Cloverdale ice rinks and more[15]

WORTH NOTINGLost court challenge over the police transition; land-swap dispute (see details)

See details and sources

Record and disputes

  • May 2024: B.C. Supreme Court dismissed the city's judicial review of the police transition. Legal invoices totalled $1,283,065.99[1][2].
  • Arena land swap: the city exchanged three city-owned parcels for Wesgroup's former Safeway site. Both sides were valued at $116.6 million with no cash changing hands; EY-Parthenon found fair market value (June 2, 2026). McCallum calls it a "bailout of a developer"; Locke denies it[3][4].
  • Police chief Norm Lipinski was removed by the police board in June 2026 and the board chair resigned. Lipinski says in court filings that Locke was behind it (his side of the story); his judicial review is pending[5][6].

Record and disputes

  • Dog-bite case: the victim says "27 stitches" was a texting typo and the actual count was about 9–11. She was fined $700 and a civil suit is under way[12].

Gaps in the promises

Voting checklist

  • Eligibility[39]: Canadian citizen aged 18 or older; resident of B.C. for at least 6 months; and either a resident of the city, or an owner of property in it for at least 30 days. Permanent residents cannot vote.
  • ID[39]: To register at the polls, bring 2 pieces of ID (at least one with a signature) showing who you are and where you live. Photo ID is not required.
  • How many votes: Richmond elects 8 councillors and 7 trustees[27]. You do not have to fill every spot.
  • School trustees: the board decides school budgets, library books and learning materials[40]. Lists are above. For advance-voting dates, check your city's notice.
